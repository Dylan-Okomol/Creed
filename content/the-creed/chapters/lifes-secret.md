## Human Beauty

From the much I have observed of myself and of humanity; it is our nature to cling to monist[^1] ideals to explain the vastness of existence. For everything we perceive, we do so individually so as naturally unified beings, we desire to unify also the world by allowing it to be all one, and one secret. 

It is a sort of cure to what Fromm, in his work *The Art of Loving*, would call the Human Condition—Separation. It is that awareness of being the object in nature that is solely subject to itself, which suggests that mans perceptions that form his truth, and therefore his will, is constrained by nature, but is not subject to it. This experience of the sublimity of our own infinity, with not the ability to naturally perceive it, has us at odds with nature.

In this respect, human beings become beings of beauty. Recognition, conceptualization then the reduction of this vastness to beautiful and ultimately simple patterns. Bringing this simplicity into functionality and using it to observe more nature and more of nature. Hence, art and the embrace of the aesthetic is an expression of the human desire for all to tend to one. 

The anxiety of human reality would be a nature that explodes into incoherent absurdity, yet, even problems that seem too large to lack beauty have time and time again, proven themselves to be but a matter of perspective.

The reversal of this is that the apparent Joy, or better said, the completion of human existence would be the realization that all in nature can be refined to one explanation and one fundamental. This becomes the ends that most of religion tends towards. That all may be one. To quote the Bible, John 17:20–23 (NKJV):

> “I do not pray for these alone, but also for those who will believe in Me through their word; that they all may be one, as You, Father, are in Me, and I in You; that they also may be one in Us, that the world may believe that You sent Me. And the glory which You gave Me I have given them, that they may be one just as We are one: I in them, and You in Me; that they may be made perfect in one, and that the world may know that You have sent Me, and have loved them as You have loved Me.”

I make my observation and judgement independent of my accordance to Christian doctrines. I only mention it as an exemplification of the desire for man to move towards a beautiful simplicity that would be, the unity of nature.

## What Nature Desires

But nature desires to be, and just be. In it's absurdity or it's beauty and perhaps it's beautiful absurdity, nature wants to be, and just be. Not to be perceived and exist as mere reference, neither constrained to human history nor be separate from them. 

Nature does not desire oneness, for in itself is already one, hence nature in fact cannot desire oneness, for what is in existence cannot will it's own existence. Nature desires to be and be itself, in its being as transitivity, for what we would call a becoming, for nature, there is nothing to become so this, movement, is not a tending towards but rather inward. 

That the perishing and falling of leaves, is a ground laid out for new life, and the cycle of destruction and rebuilding can never in it's nature be untimely, because the desire of nature is that we let it be and just be. Beware, however, that we are the one's subordinate to nature, for nature, on it's own, just is. 

As the highest gradation of that nature we are least accepting of our part in it, as the nature of humanity is to subordinate the sublime. But this very aspect about humanity, is itself nature. It is the constant overcoming of itself, that is to us historical, but in essence always timeless[^2]. 

The philosopher Friedrich Wilhelm Nietzsche forms this basis of my beliefs, with a passage on his magnum opus work *Thus Spoke Zarathustra*. He resonates;

> “And life itself confided this secret to me: ‘Behold,’ it said, ‘I am that which must always overcome itself. Indeed, you call it a will to procreate or a drive to an end, to something higher, farther, more manifold: but all this is one, and one secret.
>
> ‘Rather would I perish than forswear this; and verily, where there is perishing and a falling of leaves, behold, there life sacrifices itself—for power. That I must be struggle and a becoming and an end and an opposition to ends—alas, whoever guesses what is my will should also guess on what crooked paths it must proceed.
>
> ‘Whatever I create and however much I love it soon I must oppose it and my love; thus my will wills the overcoming. And you too, lover of knowledge, are only a path and footprint of my will; verily, my will to power walks also on the heels of your will to truth.
>
> ‘Indeed, the truth was not hit by him who shot at it with the word of the “will to existence”: that will does not exist. For, what does not exist cannot will; but what is in existence, how could that still want existence? Only where there is life is there also will: not will to life but—thus I teach you—will to power.’”[^3]

like that, I find myself with the desire to cling to this ideal in a monist way. The difference between this and other ideals, is that it intends for you to cling to it. Because however much I may create and love the borders of my philosophy, soon I must oppose it and oppose my love for it also, thus my will wills the overcoming. 

In a sense, the nature of this belief is in itself complete, through the acknowledgement of life's will to power, not moving from a state of incompleteness, but being in a state of complete overcoming.

## A Priori Judgments

All states of being are to be accepted. To embrace the transitory nature of life is to embrace life itself. Every change is to be acknowledged; even perceptively negative change, either within circumstance or within oneself, for the zone of destruction is exactly where my desire's are sacrificed, for power. 

That new perspectives, knowledge and people may spring up and cause response that inspires action. That I may overcome the object of overcoming; which is, my historical self. 

That even I as a lover of knowledge and truth, accept that the knowledge I gain, even if acquired for the sole purpose of purely gaining knowledge[^4], accept that it is merely a footprint and a path to the will to power[^5]. 

That I may be the perfect state of overcoming. That I, am inherently a perfect being in my existence in nature, and seek to embrace nature, whose nature is, that which must always overcome itself.
